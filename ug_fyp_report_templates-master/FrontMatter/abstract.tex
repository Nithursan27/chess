Chess is one of the most prominent board games of our time, undergoing huge growth in the playerbase via online accessibility. This project aims to create a computerised engine that evaluates various chess positions with MiniMax and a K-Means classifier. I implemented an evaluation function that vectorises the given chess position and produces a value representing the advantage for the player, which I primarily utilised in the search algorithm MiniMax with alpha-beta pruning to find the best possible move at a certain depth. This searching process was further enhanced by the use of the K-Means clustering algorithm. The algorithm was provided with a large clustered dataset of labelled positions based on their situations (i.e. king safety, opening type), producing a classifier that pruned branches of the MiniMax search tree when they were classified as disadvantageous. I then compared the results of this engine with Stockfish, one of the strongest open-source chess engines, to evaluate its effectiveness in producing the best moves.
